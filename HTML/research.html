Research & Resources

What Every Bioinformatician Needs to Do Research

Bioinformatics research is a mix of asking good questions, working with data, and using the right tools to turn that data into a detailed analysis and finally into a publication. For that you will need to know about core resources and tools that will make your life as a bioinformatician one great and enjoyable adventure.

Here you will find your first Research Survival Toolkit!

Step 1: Were to start? Do your research

Good research often starts by doing an in-depth analysis for related works published by other scientists. Here is the list of the most well-known and useful platforms for finding your disired articales.

  • PubMed

    • The go-to database for biomedical literature, that is reliable for its peer-reviewed papers in biology, medicine, and bioinformatics.

  • Google Scholar

    • An academic search engine that’s great for finding citations and discovering related works across disciplines.

  • Web of Science

    • A powerful (but often institution-locked) tool for citation analysis and tracking the impact of research over time.

  • ResearchGate

    • A platform where researchers share papers, preprints, and conduct discussions.

  • ORCID

    • A unique researcher ID that helps distinguish authors and track publications across platforms.

When doing research you can always look for Open access Journals that offer freely available articles that remove paywalls and make science more accessible to everyone.

Step 2: Where to find data? Find a database

Bioinformatics is data-driven science field, so knowing where to get trustworthy biological data is essential.

  • UniProt

    • A comprehensive resource for protein sequences and functional annotations, widely used in genomics and proteomics.

  • NCBI

    • A massive collection of biological databases, including genomes, sequences, and literature.

Step 3: Where to work? Know your system

Behind every Bioinformatician there hides a system that helps them throughtout thier whole research process. Linux is one of those fast and flexible systems ideal for handling large datasets and complex workflows. Once you get comfortable with it, it gives you control, transparency, and the feeling that your computer actually works with you instead of against you.

Step 4: Where are my notes? Learn your Git

Important part of every research is reproductibility which allows other scientists to trace your thought process and try to redo your analysis to confirm its credibility. Git is a version control system that keeps track of all your work in a neat and strutured way. To learn more about it, go to the next tab "GitHub & Git".

Step 5: Where to write? Choose your notebook

Choosing the right notebook will make you feel more confident and comfortable while working on your research. So play around and test out which one is the best option for you!

  • Quarto

    • A modern tool for writing reproducible documents, reports, even with the option to create websites using code and text together.

  • Jupyter

    • Interactive notebooks ideal for combining code, results, and explanations in Python or R.

Step 6: How to cite? Reference in different formats

Citations are an unavoidable part of every paper. But with the right Reference Manager you can save a lot of time!

  • Zotero

    • A free, open-source reference manager that helps you collect, organize, and cite papers effortlessly.

Step 7: What to use? Analyze with ease

  • RStudio

    • A user-friendly interface for working with R, making coding, plotting, and debugging much, much easier.

  • Tidyverse

    • A collection of R packages designed for clean, readable data analysis workflows. Commonly used packages include:

    • ggplot2 - data visualization and graphs
    • dplr - data manipulation
    • tidyr - data tiding

Step 8: Where is the end? Think, Share and Repeat

Spoiler alert: There is no real end. Science is a continuous process of thinking critically, sharing your ideas and results with others, learning from feedback, and trying again and again, each time learing something completly new.

And isn't that the whole point of research? Never stop exploring on your journey as a Scientist!
