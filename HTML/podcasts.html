Podcast Recommendations

Night Science is a series of podcasts that explores the creative and intuitive side of scientific research. The whole project aims to find the necessary and undeniably beneficial balance between hypothesis-focused Day Science and curiosity-driven Night Science.

Hosted by scientists Itai Yanai and Martin Lercher, each episode welcomes a unique guest researcher who shares their wisdom and personal journey throughout their work as a scientist. Together they discuss the creative process behind research, including great life-long advices as well as amazing stories of their many years of dedication to development of science. All in the form of podcasts available to both young and experienced scientists.

Below you fill find some of the great recommendations from the whole list of 83 podcasts!

Episode 1 – Ellen Rothenberg

Ellen Rothenberg, a Distinguished Professor of Biology at Caltech, discusses the importance of having wide and explicit knowledge that allow scientists to recognize meaningful data. Through enthusiastic metaphors she illustrates how creativity and deep understanding work together in complex biological research.

Episode 8 – Yana Bromberg

In this episode, Yana Bromberg, a Professor at Rutgers, explores the creative potential of machine learning in biology and the value of searching for “weirdness” in data. She reflects on her ability to translate ideas across disciplines and how interdisciplinary thinking ignites innovation.

Episode 11 – Ruth Lehmann

Ruth Lehmann, Director of MIT’s Whitehead Institute, shares how “careful observation” and “listening” guide her scientific discoveries. She describes creativity as an “opening of a window” and reflects on how nurturing research environments can empower scientists to do their best work.

If you are interested to learn even more about Ruth Lehmann, here you can read my essay about her podcast!

Episode 14 – Cassandra Extavour

Evolutionary biologist Bill Martin discusses how he selects research questions and navigates creativity in highly competitive scientific fields. He reflects on the dangers of overcrowded research areas and the limitations of exploratory data analysis when not guided by strong questions.

Episode 21 – Daniel Kahneman

Daniel Kahneman reflects on how dissatisfaction with existing explanations drives his creative process. The conversation explores cognitive biases, adversarial collaboration, and how scientists can suspend critical judgment to allow ideas to develop.

Episode 31 – Alfred Russel Wallace

Channeled through evolutionary biologist Andrew Berry, the episode revolves Alfred Russel Wallace and his creative process during long, solitary nights of exploration. He reflects on pattern recognition, data-driven thinking, and how intellectual freedom shaped his discoveries.

Episode 71 – Victor Ambros

Victor Ambros discusses improvisational science as a collaborative, evolving process built on shared ideas and constant re-framing of questions. He emphasizes the importance of curiosity-driven research and encouraging young scientists to challenge established thinking. The episode also touches on how artificial intelligence may complement interdisciplinary discovery.

Episode 72 – David Baker

David Baker describes how he cultivates a “communal brain” within his lab, where dense collaboration maximizes creativity and idea generation. He reflects on the role of artificial intelligence in protein design and questions whether AI itself can be creative.
